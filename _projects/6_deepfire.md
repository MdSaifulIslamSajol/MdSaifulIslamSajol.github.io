DeepFire is a multidisciplinary research project at Louisiana State University focused on predicting and preventing wildfires using artificial intelligence and data-driven modeling. The system integrates machine learning, computer vision, weather data, satellite observations, and fire-spread modeling to estimate wildfire risk weeks in advance with high accuracy. The goal is to provide early, actionable insights to fire managers so they can deploy resources proactively and reduce the impact of catastrophic wildfires.

The DeepFire team was recognized by LSU as part of the "We Build Teams That Win" initiative and became a finalist in the $11 million Wildfire XPRIZE, placing among the top teams globally in the Space-Based Wildfire Detection and Intelligence Track. In September 2026, the team received the **$100,000 Special Recognition for Operational Deliverable**, one of five Track A awardees among more than 300 teams.

I received the **LSU Tiger Team Award** as part of the "We Build Teams That Win" recognition program, for service and collaboration that significantly advanced the university's mission and goals. The award recognizes interdisciplinary teamwork and contributions to research and innovation at LSU, particularly through collaborative work on AI-driven wildfire prediction and prevention.

#### My contributions

- Contributed to DeepFire, an AI platform with linked models for fire-risk prediction, early fire detection, and fire spread forecasting.
- Developed machine learning models integrating meteorological data, satellite imagery, smoke detection (computer vision), and spatio-temporal fire spread modeling.
- Designed data fusion pipelines combining predictive risk maps with real-time environmental signals to support proactive resource allocation for fire management agencies.
- Contributed to a NASA ROSES-2025 A.13 proposal (submitted October 2026, under review) that extends DeepFire toward an operational decision-support system for the San Bernardino County Fire Protection District.
